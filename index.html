ABHA 2031

Reimagining India’s Connected Healthcare Ecosystem

A design research and speculative ecosystem project. It starts from the 2026 baseline of India’s digital health infrastructure and imagines how patient-centred interventions could make health information more accessible, interoperable and trustworthy by 2031.

Explore the projectExplore the experience

Overview

Sector: Indian digital healthcare ecosystem.
Baseline: 2026. Horizon: 2031.
Discipline: Interaction Design.
Perspective: Patient-centred.

The Record-to-Care Gap

Having an ABHA identity does not guarantee that a patient’s records are available, interoperable or usable at the point of care. A patient’s digital health information does not automatically become usable clinical information at the point of care. Patients may still carry reports, repeat their history or wait for retrieval across providers. This is a research hypothesis about implementation and patient experience, not a claim that ABDM’s architecture fails.

Research question

How might India’s digital healthcare ecosystem evolve to make healthcare information more accessible, interoperable, trustworthy and patient-centred by 2031?

Existing versus proposed. ABHA is a digital health identity, not health insurance. ABDM enables consent-based exchange; it is not a central repository, and records stay with the providers that created them. Sharing depends on consent, authorisation and law, and insurers do not automatically receive ABHA-linked records. HealthGraph, ConsentLens, DataRights Ledger and all other named innovations are speculative design proposals, not official ABDM products or deployed services.

2.1 Future STEEP horizon scan and principles

Design principles

Patient agency, interoperability, inclusivity, resilience, accountability.

Ecosystem boundary

Included: patient-controlled consent, interoperable provider links, connected histories, delegated caregiver access, offline continuity, cost transparency, affordable infrastructure, access accountability, privacy-preserving public health intelligence.
Outside: replacing doctors with autonomous AI, unrestricted record access, centralising every identifiable record, automatic insurer access to ABHA-linked data, autonomous diagnosis, surveillance use of identifiable data.

3.6 Validation and impact
No results yet. Everything below is a proposed outcome or plan. No usability, adoption or impact figures have been measured.
2026 problem2031 proposed intervention
Repeated history, paper reportsHealthGraph timeline
Hard-to-read consentConsentLens
No visibility of accessDataRights Ledger

Proposed benefits: patients gain control and fewer repeats; doctors see relevant history sooner; hospitals reduce record chasing; laboratories gain traceable delivery; caregivers get delegated, bounded access.

Usability testing plan (not yet run), five tasks: find a previous diagnostic report in HealthGraph; identify a record’s source; review and approve or decline a request in ConsentLens; find which organisation accessed the records; report an unfamiliar access event in the Ledger.

Evaluation metrics: task completion rate, time per task, navigation errors, understanding of consent, ability to identify data recipients, participant confidence and feedback.

Limitations: depends on provider participation and data quality; needs secure, reliable interoperability; consent workflows must align with governance and law; offline and multilingual features need testing; any AI summaries must stay traceable to original records and subject to clinical review.

One connected journey across three prototypes. All data is fictional demonstration data. Start in HealthGraph, then follow the new request banner.

Journey progress

    Approval does not mean delivery. The prototype shows a separate verification and exchange handoff before records appear.

    Reflection

    The project moves from understanding the 2026 ecosystem to speculating a 2031 one, then to designing the experience a patient would touch. The strongest lesson is that trust is designed through small moments: understanding a request, seeing the source of a record, and seeing who looked.

    Open opportunities: usability testing, assisted-access research with healthcare workers and legal review of the proposed consent patterns.

    ABHA 2031 is an academic design research project. Named innovations are speculative proposals, not official ABDM products. All interface data is fictional.
